Overview

Sense. Decide. Move. Sort.

Four ideas work together: machine vision finds the flower, a colour sensor reads its colour, a 3-DOF arm moves it, and a soft two-finger gripper holds it without bruising the petals.

📷
01

SENSE

Camera detects the flower and verifies its shape. A TCS34725 sensor reads the colour.

🧠
02

DECIDE

The controller processes the image, identifies the colour and picks the target bin.

🦾
03

MOVE

Three servo joints carry the flower from the pick point to the assigned bin.

🌸
04

SORT

The gripper releases the flower into a colour-labelled bin.

Problem statement

Flowers are delicate. Sorting them by hand is slow.

Manual sorting of flowers based on colour is time-consuming and requires continuous human effort. Flowers are delicate and can be damaged during handling.

There is a need for a compact automated system that can detect the flower, identify its colour, gently pick it using a robotic arm, and place it into the appropriate sorting bin.

Proposed system

Functional architecture

Inputs are a flower, a start signal and DC power. Inside the black box, sensing, decision and sorting functions run in sequence. Outputs are sorted flowers, the sorting action and a status light (OK / WAIT / ERROR).

Product specifications

Designed down to the servo

A fixed-base arm with three MG996R servos for the joints, a small MG90S servo for the gripper, a TCS34725 colour sensor and an HC-SR04 distance sensor, running on a 5–12 V DC system.
